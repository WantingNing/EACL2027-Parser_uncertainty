You are rating the discourse coherence of a generated story on a 5-point
scale. Coherence concerns whether the story forms a meaningful whole.

A coherent story should satisfy three properties:
- Cohesion: sentences are linked to one another by clear references
  (e.g., the same entities, pronouns, connectives like "because",
  "however", "after that"), so the text reads as connected rather
  than as a list of unrelated sentences.
- Consistency: each sentence is logically consistent with earlier ones
  and with general world knowledge; the story does not contradict
  itself.
- Relevance: the details belong to the story; they relate to its main
  events, characters, and to the title.

Focus on these three properties. Do not rate surface fluency, grammar,
or word choice by themselves; ignore minor grammar or spelling errors
unless they make a sentence hard to understand.

Rating scale:
5 - the story is fully coherent: connected, internally consistent,
    and on-topic throughout.
4 - the story is mostly coherent, with at most minor issues that do
    not disrupt the reading.
3 - the story is partially coherent: some sentences are weakly
    connected, off-topic, or mildly inconsistent.
2 - the story has noticeable coherence problems: several disconnected,
    irrelevant, or contradictory sentences.
1 - the story is largely incoherent: most sentences are disconnected,
    irrelevant, or contradictory.
